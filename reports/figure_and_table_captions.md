### Figure 1 — Target-relative biopsy geometry features

**Caption.**
Schematic illustration of the four target-relative geometric features derived from needle trajectory and MRI-defined target mesh coordinates. Features shown are: (i) distance from the needle midpoint to the target surface, (ii) distance from the needle midpoint to the target centroid, (iii) the fraction of the needle centreline lying inside the target volume, and (iv) a binary indicator of whether the needle trajectory intersects the target at all. The diagram is a schematic constructed from representative coordinates; it does not represent patient data and is not to scale. Axes and measurements are defined in the coordinate frame aligned to the biopsy needle.

**Key takeaway.**
Target-relative geometry operationalises how close a given biopsy core is to the radiologist-identified MRI lesion, independently of absolute prostate anatomy.

**Source:** `src/make_geometry_schematic_figure.py` (deterministic; no data input).

### Figure 2 — Compact model held-out predictive performance

**Caption.**
Held-out predictive performance of the compact `target_geometry_plus_clinical` logistic regression model on the test split (n = 2,417 cores, 120 patients). Bars show area under the ROC curve (ROC-AUC; blue) and area under the precision-recall curve (PR-AUC; green) for two endpoints: GG2+ / csPCa (ISUP Grade Group ≥ 2, baseline prevalence 10.7%) and GG3+ / high-grade (ISUP Grade Group ≥ 3, baseline prevalence 3.8%). Error bars are 95% confidence intervals from 1,000 patient-level bootstrap resamples of the test set (patients resampled with replacement, all their cores included). The compact feature set comprises four target-relative geometry features and four clinical variables (PSA, log PSA, prostate volume, PSA density). PR-AUC baseline equals the endpoint prevalence; ROC-AUC chance level is 0.5.

**Key takeaway.**
The compact model achieves ROC-AUC 0.754 [0.699–0.807] for GG2+ and 0.828 [0.751–0.892] for GG3+, with PR-AUC of 0.327 [0.230–0.461] and 0.269 [0.094–0.516] respectively, substantially above chance.

**Source:** `reports/minimal_vs_full_model_comparison.md`; generated by `src/make_compact_model_results_figures.py`.

### Figure 3 — Risk stratification enrichment among highest-scored biopsy cores

**Caption.**
Positive core prevalence among the highest-scored biopsy cores on the held-out test split, ranked by predicted probability from the compact `target_geometry_plus_clinical` logistic regression model. Results are shown for two endpoints and four groups: all test cores (baseline), and the top 20%, 10%, and 5% by predicted probability. The dashed horizontal line marks the baseline prevalence for each endpoint. Enrichment is illustrated by the annotated ratio above the top-5% bar. These results quantify the model's ability to rank biopsy cores by cancer risk, not calibrated absolute risk.

**Key takeaway.**
The top 5% of scored cores have a positive rate of 50.4% (GG2+; 4.7× baseline) and 31.4% (GG3+; 8.3× baseline), demonstrating clinically meaningful risk stratification within the held-out test set.

### Figure 4 — Cluster-robust association analysis: compact model features

**Caption.**
Forest plot of odds ratios (OR) with 95% confidence intervals for all eight features of the compact `target_geometry_plus_clinical` model, estimated by core-level logistic regression with standard errors clustered by patient. Results are shown for two endpoints (left panel: GG2+ / csPCa; right panel: GG3+ / high-grade). All valid splits are pooled (16,992 cores, 796 patients) to maximise statistical power. Continuous predictors are z-score standardised; reported ORs are per one standard deviation. `trajectory_intersects_target` is a binary (0/1) indicator and is kept un-standardised. The shaded region marks the four target-relative geometry features; the unshaded region marks the four clinical features. Filled diamonds (p < 0.05) and open circles (p ≥ 0.05) distinguish statistically significant from non-significant associations. The vertical dashed line marks OR = 1 (null hypothesis). Note that `psa_ng_ml` and `psa_density` are collinear with `log_psa_ng_ml`; their individual coefficients should be interpreted with caution (see VIF table in `reports/grouped_core_level_inference.md`).

**Key takeaway.**
Distance to target surface (GG2+: OR 0.468, p < 0.001; GG3+: OR 0.323, p < 0.001) and fraction of centreline inside target (GG2+: OR 1.331, p < 0.001; GG3+: OR 1.227, p < 0.01) show consistent, robust associations with core positivity after accounting for patient clustering.

**Source:** `reports/grouped_core_level_inference.md`, `reports/grouped_core_level_inference_cluster_robust.csv`; generated by `src/make_grouped_inference_forest_plot.py`.

### Table 1 — Dataset overview

**Caption.**
Summary of the study dataset derived from the TCIA Prostate-MRI-US-Biopsy collection, filtered to cores with confirmed coordinate matches (`label_join_status == "coord_match"`). Cores are assigned to train, validation, and test splits by patient-stratified random splitting (GroupShuffleSplit), ensuring no patient appears in more than one split. Two binary endpoints are considered: GG2+ / csPCa (ISUP Grade Group ≥ 2, i.e., Gleason score ≥ 3+4=7) and GG3+ / high-grade (ISUP Grade Group ≥ 3, i.e., Gleason score ≥ 4+3=7). Prevalences are reported per split; the test-set prevalences (GG2+: 10.7%; GG3+: 3.8%) reflect the held-out evaluation conditions for all predictive performance results.

**Key takeaway.**
The dataset comprises 16,992 cores from 796 patients; the class imbalance (10.7% and 3.8% in the test set) motivates reporting PR-AUC alongside ROC-AUC.

**Source:** `reports/grouped_core_level_inference.md` (Section 2); `reports/final_core_count_audit_summary.md`.

### Table 2 — Compact model held-out predictive performance

**Caption.**
Held-out performance of the central compact model (`target_geometry_plus_clinical` logistic regression, 8 features) on the test split (n = 2,417 cores, 120 patients), for both endpoints. Metrics reported: ROC-AUC, PR-AUC, and PR-lift (PR-AUC / test prevalence). The 95% confidence intervals are derived from 1,000 patient-level bootstrap resamples of the test set. Sensitivity, specificity, precision, and F1 are reported at the Youden-J threshold selected on the validation split. The full 14-feature `all_geometry_no_availability_plus_clinical` model is retained in the supplement as a sensitivity reference.

**Key takeaway.**
The compact model achieves ROC-AUC 0.754 (GG2+) and 0.828 (GG3+) with PR-lift 3.0 and 7.1 respectively, establishing a strong held-out predictive-performance baseline with an interpretable, parsimonious feature set.

**Source:** `reports/minimal_vs_full_model_comparison.md`; `reports/final_main_performance_table.md`.

### Table 3 — Compact vs full model performance comparison

**Caption.**
Comparison of held-out ROC-AUC and PR-AUC between the compact model (`target_geometry_plus_clinical`, 8 features) and the full geometry-clinical model (`all_geometry_no_availability_plus_clinical`, 14 features) across logistic regression, HistGradientBoosting, and XGBoost. Differences (ΔROC, ΔPR) are reported as compact minus full; negative values indicate the full model is higher. Pre-specified equivalence margins are |ΔROC| ≤ 0.010 and |ΔPR| ≤ 0.015. The full model is retained as a sensitivity/reference model; it is not claimed to be strictly superior on held-out data.

**Key takeaway.**
The compact model remains within equivalence margins for both endpoints across model families (GG2+: ΔROC = −0.002, ΔPR ≈ 0.000; GG3+: ΔROC = −0.005, ΔPR = +0.008), supporting use of the parsimonious 8-feature set as the central model.

**Source:** `reports/minimal_vs_full_model_comparison.md`; `reports/minimal_vs_full_delta_table.md`.

### Table 4 — Risk stratification: positive core prevalence in top-ranked cores

**Caption.**
Positive core prevalence among the top 5%, 10%, and 20% of test-set cores ranked by the compact model's predicted probability, compared with the baseline (overall test-set prevalence). Results are shown for logistic regression on the `target_geometry_plus_clinical` feature set. Capture rate (fraction of all positive cores recovered in the top-N%) is also reported. These figures characterise the model's discriminative ranking ability; they do not reflect calibrated absolute cancer risk and should not be used for threshold-based clinical decision-making without external validation and recalibration.

**Key takeaway.**
Concentrating inspection on the top 5% of scored cores yields a positive rate of 50.4% for GG2+ (4.7× baseline) and 31.4% for GG3+ (8.3× baseline), illustrating the practical value of model-guided biopsy core ranking.

### Table 5 — Grouped inference: cluster-robust odds ratios for compact model features

**Caption.**
Odds ratios (OR) and 95% confidence intervals from core-level logistic regression with standard errors clustered by patient (`statsmodels.GLM`, Binomial family, `cov_type='cluster'`), pooling all splits (16,992 cores, 796 patients). Both endpoints are reported in separate columns. Continuous predictors are z-score standardised (one unit = one SD); `trajectory_intersects_target` is binary (0/1) and un-standardised. Collinearity is present among both geometry and clinical predictors. `psa_ng_ml` and `log_psa_ng_ml` are near-perfectly rank-correlated because log-PSA is a monotonic transformation of PSA, while PSA density is also correlated with PSA and prostate volume. In addition, the two target-distance features are strongly correlated. Individual coefficients for correlated predictors should therefore be interpreted cautiously and not used as standalone feature-importance rankings. Concordance with GEE (exchangeable working correlation) estimates is reported in `reports/grouped_core_level_inference.md`.

**Key takeaway.**
Distance to target surface and fraction of centreline inside target are the geometry features most consistently and significantly associated with core positivity after patient clustering is accounted for; log(PSA) and prostate volume are the dominant clinical associations.

**Source:** `reports/grouped_core_level_inference.md`; `reports/grouped_core_level_inference_cluster_robust.csv`.

### Table 6 — Patient-level secondary analysis

**Caption.**
Patient-level secondary analysis comparing direct patient-level models with previous naive aggregation of core-level scores. Direct patient-level models use one row per patient, with patient labels defined as positive if at least one biopsy core is positive for the endpoint. Core-level target-geometry features are aggregated into patient-level summaries and combined with clinical variables. Patient-level prevalences are consequently higher than core-level prevalences (GG2+: 58.3%; GG3+: 25.8% on the test split). This analysis is secondary to the core-level results because patient-level prediction dilutes the local biopsy-core geometry signal.

**Key takeaway.**
Direct patient-level modelling performs similarly to naive aggregation for GG2+ and only modestly improves ROC-AUC for GG3+ while reducing PR-AUC; patient-level performance remains below core-level performance, supporting core-level modelling as the primary analytical unit.

**Source:** `reports/patient_level_direct_model_comparison.md`; `reports/final_patient_level_table.md`.

## 3. Notes on preferred wording

The following wording choices should be applied consistently across the manuscript.

- **"held-out predictive performance"** — use instead of "efficacy" or "accuracy" when referring to test-set results.
- **"risk stratification"** or **"ranking"** — use instead of "clinical decision tool" or "diagnostic system" when discussing the model's practical utility.
- **"target-relative biopsy geometry"** — use instead of vague "geometry" to be precise about which coordinate frame the features are derived from.
- **"association model"** — use for the grouped inference analysis (Table 5, Figure 4); this is an inference result, not a predictive performance result.
- **"compact model"** — use as shorthand for `target_geometry_plus_clinical` logistic regression; define fully on first mention.
- **"held-out test split"** — always specify when reporting AUC or prevalence-based results; do not conflate with validation-set numbers.

## 4. Claims to avoid

The following statements should not appear in the manuscript in any form.

- Do not claim clinical readiness, deployment suitability, or that the model should guide actual biopsy decisions.
- Do not claim calibrated absolute risk estimates: raw predicted probabilities are not well-calibrated before recalibration (BSS < 0 before Platt scaling; see `reports/final_calibration_table.md`), and no external validation has been performed.
- Do not claim core-level observations are statistically independent: intra-patient correlation is addressed by cluster-robust inference and GEE, but should be stated explicitly as a limitation.
- Do not claim target-relative geometry is independent of radiologist targeting: the MRI target used to compute geometry features is defined by the same radiologist guiding the biopsy; this circularity is a study limitation.
- Do not frame the paper as an image-only deep learning paper: the models in this phase are tabular logistic regression and tree-based models operating on pre-extracted geometric and clinical scalars.
- Do not overstate the equivalence result: the compact model is within predefined margins, not proven equivalent by a formal statistical equivalence test; the full model is retained as a sensitivity reference.
